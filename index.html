Where decisions are shared
01 / The research question

Does implementing trauma-informed principles necessarily lead to greater client autonomy, or does meaningful autonomy require changes in how decisions are shared between professionals and service users?

02 / Our starting point

Who gets to decide?

This inquiry keeps the answer open. We want to examine how principles translate into everyday decisions, and use a critical and counter-oppressive lens to question where power sits.

01

Trauma-informed principles

What does it mean to put safety, trust, collaboration and choice into practice? Which actions would demonstrate these commitments?

02

Meaningful autonomy

Can a person shape their goals, decline an option and revisit a decision? How might we distinguish an offered choice from real influence?

03

Power in practice

Who defines the available options, controls resources and has the final say? What would need to change for decisions to be shared?

03 / Interactive scenarios

What would you do?

Explore three fictional practice situations. Choose a response, examine who holds decision-making power, then compare alternatives. These are discussion prompts, not a scored assessment or practice guidance.
