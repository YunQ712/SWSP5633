What Is Trauma?

Explore the definition below alongside your initial understanding.

Your Understanding

SAMHSA's Definition

Individual trauma results from an event, series of events, or set of circumstances that is experienced by an individual as physically or emotionally harmful or life threatening and that has lasting adverse effects on the individual’s functioning and mental, physical, social, emotional, or spiritual well-being.

Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA, 2014, p. 7).

Event

What happened?

Traumatic events may involve actual or threatened physical or psychological harm, including violence, disasters, or severe neglect. They may occur as a single event or repeatedly over time.

Experience

How was it experienced?

An individual's perception and interpretation of an event influence whether it is experienced as traumatic. This experience may be shaped by cultural beliefs, social support, developmental stage, and feelings of fear, shame, or powerlessness.

Effect

What impact did it have?

Trauma can have lasting adverse effects on an individual's emotional, physical, cognitive, and social functioning. These effects may emerge immediately or develop over time.

Trauma is therefore not defined only by what happens, but also by how an individual experiences the event and its lasting impact (SAMHSA, 2014).

A moment to reflect

Looking at both definitions, is there anything you would add to or reconsider about your initial understanding?

Reference: Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration. (2014). SAMHSA's concept of trauma and guidance for a trauma-informed approach (HHS Publication No. SMA 14-4884). View the report.

Submodule 2 · Information cards

Types and Sources of Trauma

Trauma can take different forms depending on the nature, duration, and context of traumatic experiences.

Types of Trauma

Acute Trauma

Results from a single distressing event, such as a serious accident or natural disaster.

Chronic Trauma

Develops through repeated or prolonged exposure to distressing experiences, such as ongoing violence or abuse.

Complex Trauma

Involves multiple or repeated traumatic experiences, often occurring within interpersonal relationships during childhood, potentially affecting development and attachment.

Sources: National Child Traumatic Stress Network (NCTSN, 2025); Levenson (2020).

Common Sources of Trauma

Abuse & Neglect

Physical, emotional, or sexual abuse and neglect.

Domestic & Community Violence

Domestic and community violence.

War, Displacement & Disasters

War, forced displacement, and natural disasters.

Accidents, Illness & Loss

Serious accidents, life-threatening illness, or sudden loss.

Discrimination & Oppression

Discrimination, racism, and other forms of systemic oppression.

Sources: SAMHSA (2014); NCTSN (2025).

These experiences may overlap, and their effects vary between individuals.
