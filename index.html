Fictional composite scenario. Autism is not presented as trauma; Alex’s identity, access needs and past experiences remain distinct. The story does not predict how a real person would respond.

Learn the foundations

Four modules. One practice question.

Use the learning cards alongside either scenario. Explore them in any order, then return to a decision and try another response.

01

Understanding trauma

  • What trauma means and where it can come from
  • Impacts on people and relationships
  • Loss, grief, culture and resilience

Aim: consider different meanings without explaining every behaviour through trauma.

02

Understanding trauma-informed practice

  • Why trauma-informed practice matters in social work
  • SAMHSA’s Six Principles and the Four Rs
  • How it differs from trauma-specific intervention

Aim: connect a clear practice framework to everyday decisions.

03

Applying practice principles

  • Communication, safety and trust
  • Assessment and responding to disclosure
  • Professional power and critical reflection

Aim: practise concrete responses while noticing whose knowledge shapes the record.

04

Interactive simulations

  • Choose a setting and make practice decisions
  • Compare possible responses and their effects
  • Reflect, revisit and try another path

Aim: examine professional judgement without reducing complex practice to a score.

SWSP5633 · Student group project

Does implementing trauma-informed principles necessarily lead to greater client autonomy, or does meaningful autonomy require changes in how decisions are shared between professionals and service users?

What this prototype explores

Rapport building, assessment, care planning and shared decision-making are the four stages of this fictional encounter. The reflection prompts invite a critical and counter-oppressive reading of professional authority and organisational constraints.

Client replies are authored possibilities, not predictions of how real people respond. Different people may respond differently to the same words.

Evidence, scope and group contributions

The scenario, interpretations and dialogue are a draft for group and mentor review. Verified literature, service-user perspectives, references and individual contributions still need to be added before academic submission. No real client records are used.

The youth work and NDIS scenarios are fictional learning exercises. The school setting is a proposed extension. The NDIS scenario does not define current eligibility or funding rules.
